1.4 Prime Factors

  • A natural number greater than 1 that is not a prime number is called a composite number
  • The first few composite numbers are: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18
  • Unlike a prime number, a composite number can be written as a product of two factors, neither of which is 1
  • Composite numbers can often be written as products of even more factors
  • for example: 360 can be broke down into a product of more factors in the following way
  • the factors 20 and 18 are composite, so they can be written as prodcts of two factors, neither of which is 1
  • you can continue the process until all the numbers at the ends of the tree are prime numbers, the prime numbers at he end of the tree are circles
  • The fundamental theromn of arithmetic is the reason why the prime numbers can be thought of as the building blocks of the natural numbers
  • When you want to write a number as a product of prime factors it is sometimes helpful to be systtematic. Start writing the number as the smallest possible prime number x a number
  • You can use prime factorisation to help you find the lowerst common multiples and highest common factors of setts of numbers
  • for example find the lowest common multiple and highest common factor of 84 and 280
    • Write out the prime factorisations, with a coloum for each different prime
      • 84 = 2 x 2 x 3 x 7 = 2^2 x 3^1 x 7^1
      • 280 = 2 x 2 x 2 x 5 x 7 = 2^3 x 5^1 x 7^1
    • To find the LCM, circle the highest power of the prime in each column
      • LCM = 2^3 x 3^1 x 5^1 x 7^1 = 2520
